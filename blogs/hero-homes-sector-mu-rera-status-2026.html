Current RERA Status: What "Coming Soon" Actually Means

We list our RERA status honestly, and it reads the same way across every page on this site: "RERA registration is currently under process and listed as 'Coming Soon.'" Here's what that means in practice, so there's no ambiguity:

  • A pre-launch stage is a legitimate, common phase in NCR real estate — developers often open early interest and indicative pricing ahead of formal registration. It is not the same as being registered.
  • Under RERA (Real Estate Regulation and Development Act, 2016), any project with more than 8 units or over 500 sq. m. of land must be registered with the state authority — UP RERA in this case — before it can be formally advertised, marketed, or sold, and before binding bookings can be taken.
  • Once registered, a project must publicly disclose its RERA number, sanctioned building plans, promoter details, the legally committed possession date, and maintain a dedicated escrow account holding 70% of buyer collections — protections that only formally apply once the registration is live.
  • We will update this page and our FAQs the moment our RERA registration number is issued. Until then, treat any registration number claims from unofficial resellers, aggregators, or third-party listing sites for this project with caution.

What Happens Legally If a Project Sells Without RERA Registration?

This is worth understanding in plain terms, because it's the exact protection RERA registration status is meant to signal. Under Sections 3 and 59 of the RERA Act, a promoter who advertises, markets, books, sells, or invites persons to purchase a plot, apartment, or building without first registering the project with the state authority can face a penalty of up to 10% of the estimated project cost — and, for repeated or continued violation, imprisonment of up to three years, or a further penalty, or both.

This isn't a reason to assume the worst about any specific pre-launch project — most developers, including established groups like Hero Realty, work through registration in the normal course of pre-launch marketing. It's a reason to understand why a genuine "Coming Soon" disclosure (rather than a vague or absent one) is actually a positive signal: it means the project is being marketed transparently while registration is completed, instead of pretending a number already exists.

Red Flags to Watch for During the Pre-RERA Stage

Whether you're evaluating Hero Homes Greater Noida or any other pre-launch project in Sector MU, Greater Noida, or the wider NCR region, these are the warning signs that should make you pause before paying anything:

  • A fabricated or "borrowed" RERA number. Some resellers quote a RERA number from a different phase, tower, or even an unrelated project. Always cross-check the number directly on up-rera.in — never take a screenshot or verbal confirmation at face value.
  • Pressure to pay before documentation. A legitimate developer will provide a cost sheet, payment schedule, and application form before asking for money — not after.
  • No clarity on the promoter's legal entity name. The name receiving your payment should match the promoter name that will eventually appear on the RERA certificate. If a channel partner or agent's personal account is involved, that's a red flag.
  • Vague or shifting possession timelines. Pre-RERA, a possession date is indicative. Post-registration, it becomes a legally committed date tied to penalty clauses if missed — so the two carry very different weight.
  • No written refund policy for pre-launch tokens. If a project changes its sanctioned layout, unit size, or pricing after RERA registration compared to what you were shown at booking, you should have a clear, written right to a full refund.

None of this is unique to Hero Homes Greater Noida — it's the standard due-diligence checklist for any pre-launch purchase in Greater Noida, Noida, or Ghaziabad, and it's exactly the checklist our own sales team expects buyers to run through with us.

RERA Benefits You Get Once Hero Homes Greater Noida Is Registered

It's worth knowing what changes for you the moment a formal RERA number is issued, since this is the protection window every buyer should be tracking:

  • Escrow-protected payments: 70% of the funds collected from buyers must be deposited in a separate escrow account and can only be withdrawn for construction and land costs, certified by an engineer, architect, and chartered accountant — reducing the risk of fund diversion to other projects.
  • A legally binding possession date: Once registered, the promoter is bound to the completion date declared to RERA, with interest payable to buyers for delays under Section 18 of the Act.
  • Sanctioned, public layout plans: The exact building plan, layout, and unit specifications become part of the public RERA filing, so what's sold matches what's approved.
  • Quarterly progress disclosures: Registered promoters must update construction progress on the RERA portal every quarter, giving buyers an independent way to track development without relying solely on the developer's own updates.
  • A formal grievance and compensation route: Registered projects fall under the RERA Authority and Appellate Tribunal's jurisdiction, giving buyers a faster, more specialized dispute-resolution path than a standard civil suit.
